Why look past InShot on desktop

InShot has no native Mac app. On an Apple silicon Mac the App Store will install the iPad version (InShot's App Store listing, read 2 November 2026) (that is the phone interface on a bigger screen, not a desktop editor) and the "InShot for PC" downloads are Android emulators, which are slow, clunky, and defeat the point of working on a big screen with real files. And even on your phone, the free version puts a watermark on what you export and shows ads. InShot’s listing says a Pro subscription removes both, and the US App Store sells Pro at $4.99 a month, $19.99 a year or $49.99 lifetime. For a lot of creators the Pro upsell and the watermark are the whole reason they go looking for something else.

Where InShot still wins (being honest)

InShot is a mature, phone-native creative editor and it's fair to say so. It has a manual timeline with keyframes, speed ramps and picture-in-picture, more than 1,000 stickers, text templates, transitions, filters and a music library, plus AI tools for captions, background removal and cutting out silences, and it lives in your pocket so you can cut a clip on the bus. If your workflow is "grab a trend, drop in stickers and captions, post from my phone," InShot is built for exactly that. Crisp deliberately doesn't try to be a pocket sticker studio.

Can you run InShot on a Mac at all?

This is the question behind the search, and most comparison pages answer it too confidently in one direction or the other. Here is what can actually be established.

InShot’s own site (inshot.com, read 2 November 2026) offers the app on three stores: the App Store, Google Play and Huawei AppGallery. It names no Mac or desktop version. So there is no native Mac build to download, and anyone telling you to “download InShot for Mac” is describing something the developer does not publish.

That is not the same as “it cannot run on your Mac”. Apple Silicon Macs can run iPhone and iPad apps directly, but only when the developer leaves that option switched on, and it is the developer’s choice per app. InShot leaves it on: its App Store listing (read 2 November 2026) is marked “Designed for iPad” and says InShot runs on a Mac with an Apple M1 chip or newer, on at least version 13.0 of macOS.

What you get is the iPad app in a window: a touch-first interface driven by a mouse, working from files you have copied onto the machine, with the same feature set it has on a handset. That may be exactly enough: InShot is a good editor and a lot of finished video has been cut on it. What it is not is a desktop tool built for a large timeline, a 4K export, or footage that never came off a phone in the first place.
