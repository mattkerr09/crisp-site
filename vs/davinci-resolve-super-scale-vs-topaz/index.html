Super Scale: the upscaler inside your editor

Super Scale is part of DaVinci Resolve Studio. Blackmagic's Studio page lists SuperScale among the extra Resolve FX that Studio adds, next to AI UltraNR noise reduction and de-interlacing, and describes it as "high quality image uprezzing" for making 4K and 8K images from HD sources (read 2 November 2026). Version 21 adds AI UltraSharpen, which Blackmagic says to use with upscaled video to sharpen it at the higher resolution.

The case for it is simple. If you cut in Resolve Studio, it is already paid for, and you never leave the project you are editing. There is no second app and no export round trip.

The case against is also simple. It is one tool in a professional suite. If all you want is to make an old clip bigger, Resolve is a lot of program to learn for one feature, and the free version will not do it.

Topaz Video: the specialist you rent

Topaz Video does one job, which is making footage look better. Its pricing page names more than a dozen video models, from Proteus and Iris to Apollo and Chronos, plus Stabilization and SDR to HDR. On a Mac, Topaz requires macOS 13, an M-series chip and 16 GB of memory, and Topaz recommends 32 GB or more. Intel Macs are not supported (all read 2 November 2026).

Two things are worth knowing before you pay. It is a subscription: $299 a year, or $254 for the first year in Topaz Video's sale, read 11 Nov 2026, so the regular price is charged again each year after the first-year sale. And on a Mac, the Starlight Sharp, HQ and Fast models run in Topaz's cloud, not on your machine; its system requirements page says they are not yet supported on Macs for local processing. The $59 monthly plan includes 25 cloud credits a month, and they do not roll over (read 2 November 2026).

The third option, timed on my Mac

I build Crisp, so read this part knowing that. I don't have Resolve Studio or a Topaz plan on this Mac, so these are Crisp's numbers only, not a race. I took 8 seconds of the Viking 1 lander's first colour view of Mars (NASA/JPL imagery from a 1987 VHS release, 640×480 at 29.97 fps) and ran Crisp's Enhance on it at 2× and at 4×.

Each time is from the moment the job started to the finished file. Other work was running on this Mac at the time, so a quiet one may be a little faster, and a MacBook Air will take longer. The videos stayed on this Mac and no account was used.

Two things happened that I didn't ask for. 2× took as long as 4×, because on tape and SD footage Crisp uses its larger detail model for both. And Crisp found the 3:2 pulldown left by the film-to-tape transfer and took it out, so the 29.97 fps tape came back at 23.976 fps, the film's own rate.

One thing I did have to ask for. The archive copy had been squeezed into small square compression blocks before I ever got it, and Enhance on its own kept them. Typing "enhance it and remove the compression blocks" into Crisp's prompt bar adds a deblock pass before the upscale. That is the third row, and the second half of the crop below.

Which one to pick

Prices in this list are as read on 2 November 2026.

  • You already pay for Resolve Studio: use Super Scale. There is nothing else to buy.
  • You use the free Resolve and upscale now and then: Studio at $295, or $236 while the sale lasts, buys Super Scale and the rest of Studio. Crisp costs less and does this one job, with simple edits.
  • Your footage is damaged and the work pays for itself: Topaz, at $299 a year, or $254 for the first year in Topaz Video's sale, read 11 Nov 2026, has the most models of the three.
  • You want it on your Mac, paid for once, with your videos kept on your computer: Crisp. The free copy exports with a small mark, so try a short piece of your worst clip before you spend anything.

Questions people ask

Is DaVinci Resolve Super Scale free?

No. Blackmagic lists Super Scale among the tools DaVinci Resolve Studio adds over the free version (read 2 November 2026).

Is DaVinci Resolve free for Mac?

The standard version is. Blackmagic says the free version works with virtually all 8-bit formats at up to 60 fps, and Studio adds 10-bit video, up to 120 fps and resolutions beyond 4K (read 2 November 2026).

Does Super Scale work on an Apple Silicon Mac?

Yes. DaVinci Resolve Studio runs on Mac, Windows and Linux, and Blackmagic says it is optimised for Metal and Apple Silicon (read 2 November 2026).

Is Super Scale as good as Topaz?

I haven't run both on the same clip, so I won't call it. The fair test is your own footage in each, watched at full size while it plays. A paused frame hides the flicker that separates upscalers.
