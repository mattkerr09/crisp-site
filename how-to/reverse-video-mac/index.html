How to reverse a video on a Mac

By Matt Kerr, who makes Crisp in Grand Rapids · published 2026-07-05 · last updated 2026-11-11
Quick answer

Drop the clip into Crisp, select it and pick Reverse in the Tools panel, or type “reverse it” and press Do it. Reverse plays the whole clip backwards, sound included, at the same length. Boomerang plays it forward and then back, so it comes out about twice as long. It all runs offline on your Mac. The limit is memory, not seconds: Crisp reverses up to 1.9 minutes of 1080p at 30 fps, or 29 seconds of 4K (the budget in its own code, worked out below).

The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and type what you want:

  • reverse it
  • play it backwards
  • reverse the clip, or just rewind
  • make it a boomerang (or ping-pong)

Press Do it and Crisp reverses the clip on your timeline. Say boomerang or ping pong and it picks the boomerang mode; say reverse, backwards, rewind or play it back and it picks straight reverse.

It's careful about intent, too. A plain description of motion isn't a command: "the dancer steps backwards" or "the camera pans back and forth" describes what's already happening in the shot, so Crisp doesn't jump to reversing the whole clip over it. Only an actual instruction ("reverse it", "make it a boomerang") routes to the Reverse lane. And if you ask for a bigger edit in the same breath ("reverse it, upscale to 4K") Crisp splits the request and plans both as two steps, so nothing gets dropped.

The manual way: the Reverse tool

  1. Open Crisp and drop in your video

    Get the free Crisp app for Mac and drag your clip onto the window. Every step runs on your Mac; your videos never leave it.

  2. Open the Reverse tool

    In Crisp, select the clip on your timeline and find Reverse in the Tools panel. It's for video clips, so it doesn't appear when a photo is selected.

  3. Choose Reverse or Boomerang

    Pick Reverse or Boomerang in its menu. Reverse plays the whole clip backwards with the audio reversed along with it. Boomerang plays it forward then backward, seamlessly. The clip ends up about twice as long, with the audio kept in sync across both halves.

  4. Press Apply

    Crisp reverses the clip on-device with hardware encoding, puts it on your timeline and saves it beside your original, ready to post.

Reverse vs Boomerang, which one you want

Reverse is the full rewind: the entire clip runs backwards, audio and all, and stays the same length. Boomerang (also called ping-pong) is the loop-y Instagram effect. It plays forward, then plays the reversed copy right after, so a one-second clip becomes a roughly two-second forward-and-back loop. Because Crisp reverses and re-times the audio to match the video, the boomerang's sound lines up with the picture instead of drifting out of sync the way a naive concat would.

A note on long and 4K clips, honest limits

Reversing isn't like a trim or a crop that streams through the file. To play a clip backwards, every decoded frame has to be held in memory at once, so the real cost is total pixels, resolution × frame rate × length, not just how many seconds long it is. A four-minute 4K clip is short in seconds but enormous in buffered frames.

So Crisp puts a real number on it rather than a vague one. The budget is about 10 GiB of buffered frames, and the arithmetic is width × height × 1.5 bytes × frames per second × seconds: doubled for a boomerang, which holds the forward and the backward halves at once. At 30 fps that works out to:

720×480 (DVD, camcorder)11.5 min5.8 min
1280×7204.3 min2.2 min
1920×10801.9 min1.0 min
3840×2160 (4K)29 sec14 sec

Those are the limits the app itself computes, from the same constants the check is written against, not estimates. Note how badly duration alone would describe them: a four-minute 4K clip is shorter than the DVD clip that sails through, and would buffer roughly 83 GB. There is also a flat 30-minute backstop for very small, very long files. Halve the numbers above for 60 fps footage, double them for 15.

Past the budget Crisp declines it with a friendly message and asks you to trim it shorter or lower the resolution first. It refuses rather than crash. Reverse and boomerang are short-clip social effects, so short clips are exactly the sweet spot. If you've got a long one, run it through Enhance or trim it down first, then reverse the part you actually need.

What Crisp's Reverse is, and isn't

Crisp's Reverse tool does one thing cleanly: it plays your clip backwards, or loops it forward-and-back, with the audio handled correctly and hardware encoding on your Mac. That covers the overwhelming majority of "reverse video" searches: the rewind gag, the boomerang, the satisfying un-pour.

What it deliberately doesn't try to be is a timeline NLE. There's no speed-ramping, no keyframe control, no multi-track timeline: if you need to reverse only the middle three seconds with an ease-in and a ramp, that's a job for Premiere’s Time Remapping or Final Cut’s variable speed tools (both in their makers’ own guides, read 2 November 2026). We'd rather tell you that than pretend. For a clean full-clip reverse or a boomerang, though, Crisp is the fastest, most private way to do it on a Mac.

Playing it backwards, in detail

How do I reverse a video on a Mac for free?

Use Crisp: drop your clip in, select the clip and use Reverse in the Tools panel and press Apply (or type "reverse it" and press Do it). It plays the whole clip backwards, audio included, entirely on your Mac, with no account, no upload.

Does it reverse the audio too?

Yes. Reverse plays everything backwards, sound included. For a boomerang, the audio runs forward then reversed, kept in sync with the video so the two halves line up exactly.

What's the difference between reverse and boomerang?

Reverse plays the clip backwards and keeps it the same length. Boomerang (ping-pong) plays it forward then backward, so the result is about twice as long: the loop-y Instagram effect.

Why did Crisp decline my long/4K clip?

Reversing has to hold every frame in memory, so very long or high-resolution clips can't be reversed safely. Crisp declines them with a friendly message instead of crashing: trim the clip shorter or lower the resolution and try again.

Can I reverse a photo?

No. Reverse and boomerang are video-only, so the Reverse tool doesn't appear when a photo is selected.
