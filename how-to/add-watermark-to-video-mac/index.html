Quick answer

Crisp burns it in at 18% of the video’s width, inset 5% of its height from the edge, on every clip whatever the logo file’s own size, and keeps the audio. It runs offline on your Mac. The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and type what you want:

  • add my logo: top-right by default
  • add a watermark: same thing, in words
  • watermark this: the same, in two words
  • put my logo in the corner, corner placement

Pick your logo once with Choose image… in the Logo tool. After that, press Do it and Crisp burns it into the clip on your timeline, in the corner you named (top right if you didn't). Ask before a logo is picked and Crisp says so and points you at that button.

The manual way: the Logo tool

  1. Open Crisp and drop in your video

    Get the free Crisp app for Mac and drag your clip onto the window. Nothing is uploaded: the whole job runs on your Mac.

  2. Open the Logo tool

    In Crisp, select the clip on your timeline and find Logo in the Tools panel.

  3. Choose your logo image

    Click Choose image… and pick your logo. A PNG with transparency looks best, so only the mark shows and there's no box around it.

  4. Set the position

    Pick a position: bottom right, bottom left, top right, top left or centre. Crisp sizes the logo at 18% of the video width on every clip regardless of the file's own resolution, so a 200 px logo and a 2000 px logo land identically.

  5. Press Apply

    Crisp burns the logo in on-device with hardware encoding, keeps the audio untouched, puts the result on your timeline and saves it beside your original. Several clips? Pro: watermark every clip in a project in one go with Apply to every clip (or type “watermark all of these”). The free version does one clip at a time, and the logo you picked stays picked.

The same size on every clip, and never off the edge

Crisp scales your logo to the size you choose as a share of the video width, so it looks consistent whether your logo file is tiny or huge, and whether the clip is 1080p or 4K. Placement is computed from the real, rotation-aware frame (so a portrait clip shot on a phone can't end up with a logo wider than the picture) and the logo's transparency is respected, so you get the mark and nothing else.

Logo watermark vs text watermark

Crisp has two ways to stamp a video, and they're deliberately different:

  • Watermark places an image: your logo PNG. Use it for a brand mark or badge in a corner.
  • Captions burns in styled text you can position and time. Use it for a name, handle, or © line when you don't have a logo image.

Rule of thumb: a picture → Watermark; words → Captions.

Where it sits, and why the margin is a percentage

There are seven anchor points: the four corners, top centre, bottom centre and dead centre. A watermark defaults to the top right and a caption to the bottom centre, which is simply where each is conventionally read and least likely to collide with a face or a lower-third.

The inset from that edge is expressed as a percentage of the video's height (5% by default, and anything from 0 to 25) rather than as a fixed number of pixels. This matters more than it sounds. A 40-pixel margin is a comfortable gap on a 1080p clip and an invisible hairline on a 4K one, so a pixel margin silently changes the design every time the resolution does. A percentage keeps the same visual spacing at any size, which is also what makes it safe to put the same logo on a whole set of mixed footage and get a consistent result. Pro: watermark every clip in a project in one go.

Because the position is an expression evaluated against the real frame (not a baked coordinate) a corner watermark stays in its corner on portrait, landscape and square clips alike, with no per-clip adjustment.

Watermarks, in detail

How do I add a watermark to a video on a Mac for free?

Use Crisp: drop your clip in, select the clip, use Logo in the Tools panel, choose your logo image and a corner, and press Apply. It burns the logo in and keeps the audio, entirely on your Mac, with no account, no upload.

Can I choose where the watermark goes?

Yes. Bottom right, bottom left, top right, top left or centre, at 18% of the video width. Placement uses the real rotation-aware frame, so a portrait phone clip never gets a logo wider than the picture.

Will the logo keep its transparency?

Yes. Crisp respects a PNG's alpha channel, so only the mark is drawn: no opaque box behind it. Export your logo as a transparent PNG for the cleanest look.

Can I add a text watermark instead of a logo?

For text (a name, handle, or © line) use the Caption tool, which burns in styled text you can place and time. The Logo tool is for a logo image.

Does watermarking re-compress the video?

It re-encodes the picture, but Crisp hardware-encodes at high quality on Apple Silicon and writes the file locally and atomically, so the loss is negligible and you can check the result immediately.
