The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and type it:

  • add a white border: the classic framed look
  • put a black border around it, bold, high-contrast
  • add a thick red border: colour + thickness in one go
  • add a thin border: a subtle edge

Press Do it and Crisp frames the clip on your timeline in the colour and thickness you named. Borders have no menu of their own: the words are the settings.

The whole picture stays: nothing is cropped

A border in Crisp grows the canvas and paints the margin around the outside, so every pixel of your video is still there inside the frame. That's the opposite of Crop, which trims the edges off. Thin, medium and thick are roughly 3%, 6% and 12% of the shorter side, so the border scales sensibly whatever the resolution.

Border vs Crop vs Reframe

Three edge-related tools, three different jobs:

  • Border: add a coloured margin around the video (frame it). That's this guide.
  • Crop: trim the edges off (remove black bars, punch in). See crop a video.
  • Reframe: fit the clip to a new aspect (e.g. square or 9:16) with a blurred fill. See make it vertical.

How thick each border is, and what it does to the file

The three sizes are proportions, not pixel counts: 3%, 6% and 12% of the frame's shorter side. That is the whole reason a border looks the same on a phone clip and a 4K one, and why you never have to work out a number yourself.

SourceThinMediumThick
1920×108032px → 1984×114464px → 2048×1208130px → 2180×1340
1080×1920 (vertical)32px → 1144×198464px → 1208×2048130px → 1340×2180
3840×2160 (4K)64px → 3968×2288130px → 4100×2420258px → 4356×2676

Read the second column of each row carefully, because it is the thing people are surprised by: a border grows the canvas. Your picture is not cropped or shrunk to make room. The frame gets bigger and the original sits in the middle at its full size, so nothing is lost and nothing is softened. If you need the output to stay exactly 1920×1080, add the border and then crop back, or use Reframe instead, which fits the picture into a target shape rather than growing the canvas.

Every dimension is rounded to an even number, because 4:2:0 video (which is nearly all video) stores colour at half resolution in each direction and cannot have an odd side. And the colour is chosen from a fixed list of seven (white, black, gray, red, blue, pink and yellow) rather than a free text field, so no arbitrary string is ever handed to the renderer.
