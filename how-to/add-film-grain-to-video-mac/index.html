How to add film grain to a video on a Mac

A little film grain is the fastest way to turn a clean, over-smooth digital clip into something that feels shot on film — warmer, more analog, more cinematic. Here's how to add film grain to a video on a Mac in one tap, 100% offline: no account, no upload.

Grain is added on purpose — it's not the same as noise you want gone

Film grain is a controlled amount of speckle added for style. That's the exact opposite of Crisp's denoise, which strips out unwanted noise. If a clip already looks too grainy, ask Crisp to remove grain instead — it routes to the clean-up lane, not this one.

Stack it for a full film look

Grain lives in the same Color lane as Crisp's one-tap looks and the vignette, so you can build a complete retro-film treatment in a single pass: a moody or warm grade, a soft vignette, and film grain on top. It's the "shot on something nicer than a phone" combination: all on-device.

Grain size has to match the frame

The most common mistake is grain that's too fine. Real film grain scales with the negative, so 16mm grain is chunky and 35mm is tight — but digital grain applied at 4K often ends up so small it vanishes entirely after the platform re-encodes your upload. If the effect disappears on YouTube, the grain was too fine, not too weak.

Grain also interacts badly with heavy compression. Because it changes every frame, an encoder spends bitrate describing noise instead of your picture. Add it modestly and export at a decent bitrate, or the platform will turn your grain into blocking.

Grain belongs on the whole frame, at the end

Apply grain as the last step, after colour and after any upscaling. Grain added before an upscale gets enlarged along with the picture, turning fine speckle into visible blobs, and a denoiser running later will try to remove the grain you deliberately added and smear the image doing it.

Digital footage needs less than you think

Film grain reads as authentic because real film had it everywhere, including in the highlights. Most digital grain plugins apply an even layer across the frame, which is close enough — but the amount that looked right on a cinema screen is far too much on a phone. Set it while watching at the size people will actually watch, not at 200% zoom on a 27-inch display where you're hunting for the effect.

One practical test: export a short section, watch it on your phone, and if you can't tell the grain is there but the footage feels less clinically sharp, you got it right.
