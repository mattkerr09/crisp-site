The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and type the cut you want. It understands the way people actually describe a trim:

  • trim to the first 10 seconds, keep the opening
  • cut to 5 to 10 seconds, an exact range
  • keep the last 30 seconds: worked out from the clip's length
  • cut everything after 30 seconds: an open-ended tail cut
  • trim everything before 10 seconds: drop the intro
  • trim off the last 5 seconds, chop the end

Press Do it and Crisp cuts the clip on your timeline. And because it understands the whole sentence, an edit you ask for alongside the cut isn't lost: "trim to the first 5 seconds and make it warmer" keeps the warm colour grade, and "cut to 10 seconds and upscale to 4K" keeps the upscale. The cut never silently throws away the bigger job.

The manual way: In and Out

  1. Open Crisp and drop in your video

    Get the free Crisp app for Mac and drag your clip onto the window. Every step runs on your Mac; your videos never leave it.

  2. Select the clip

    Click your clip on the timeline. Its In and Out appear under Clip. A trim is a fast standalone cut: it doesn't upscale or re-grade, so it's quick.

  3. Set In and Out

    Type the In and Out in seconds, or play to the spot and press In ⟸ playhead or Out ⟸ playhead.

  4. Export

    In and Out are live settings: Crisp cuts the clip frame-accurately when you press Export video, on-device with hardware encoding, trims the audio to the same range so it stays in sync, and saves the result beside your original. A typed trim cuts the clip straight away instead.

Why "frame-accurate" matters

A lot of quick trimmers do a stream copy. They don't re-encode, so the cut snaps to the nearest keyframe, which can be a second or two away from where you asked. That's why a "cut at 10 seconds" sometimes lands at 8 or 12. Crisp re-encodes the cut so it lands exactly on the second you set, and applies the same range to the audio so nothing drifts out of sync. On Apple Silicon that re-encode is hardware-accelerated, so it's still fast. You get precision without the wait.

What Crisp's Trim is, and isn't

Trimming does one thing cleanly: it cuts your clip to a single time range, frame-accurately, audio in sync, on your Mac. That covers the overwhelming majority of "trim video" and "cut video" searches: keeping the good bit, dropping the intro, topping-and-tailing before you post.

For more than one range, the timeline does the rest: split a clip at the playhead (press S), remove the pieces you don't want, and line up separate clips in the order you like before you export. And if what you really want is to cut out the dead air from a long recording, Crisp has a smarter tool for that: ask it to "trim the dead air" or "condense this", and it uses the Montage engine to find and drop the boring stretches automatically. For a clean single-range cut, though, In and Out are the fastest, most private way to do it on a Mac.

Trimming questions

How do I trim a video on a Mac for free?

Use Crisp: drop your clip in, type something like "trim to the first 10 seconds" in the box above the preview and press Do it, or set the clip's In and Out and export. It cuts the clip frame-accurately, keeps the audio in sync, and runs entirely on your Mac with no account, no upload.

Is the cut frame-accurate, or does it snap to keyframes?

Frame-accurate. Crisp re-encodes the cut so it lands exactly where you asked, rather than doing a stream copy that snaps to the nearest keyframe and can drift by a second or two.

Can I keep the last part of a clip, or everything after a point?

Yes. Crisp understands open-ended cuts like "keep the last 30 seconds", "cut everything after 30 seconds" and "trim everything before 10 seconds". It works the exact range out from the clip's length for you.

Can I trim and change the look at the same time?

Yes. Ask for both in one sentence, "trim to the first 5 seconds and make it warmer", and Crisp keeps the colour grade instead of dropping it, doing the cut and the enhancement in one pass.

What if I want to cut the boring bits out of a long recording?

That's a different job than a single-range trim. Ask Crisp to "trim the dead air" or "condense this" and it uses its Montage engine to find and drop the slow stretches automatically.

Splitting a clip

Split or trim? Split cuts one clip into two at the playhead, and both stay on the timeline (press S, or right-click and choose Split here). Trim shortens a clip from its start or end without cutting it in two, which is what the rest of this page is about. Cut on motion, not on silence. The instinct is to cut where the audio stops, but cuts usually feel more natural mid-movement, as a hand starts to rise or someone turns, because the eye is already tracking the motion and rides through the edit; a cut on a dead frame draws attention to itself.

Splitting is not destructive. It never touches the file on disk: you are setting in and out points on a reference, so you can split the same source a dozen times and the original stays exactly as it was. An accidental split costs nothing: undo it, or delete the piece and drag the remaining edge back out.
