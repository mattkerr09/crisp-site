The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and describe the problem. It understands the way people actually say it:

  • rotate it right: 90° clockwise
  • rotate left: 90° counter-clockwise
  • my video is sideways: Crisp turns it upright
  • my video is upside down: a 180° flip
  • mirror it (or flip it horizontally), un-reverse a selfie clip

Press Do it and Crisp turns the clip on your timeline. And if you ask for a bigger edit in the same breath, "rotate it right and upscale to 4K", Crisp splits the request and plans both as two steps, so nothing gets dropped.

The manual way: the Rotate tool

  1. Open Crisp and drop in your video

    Get the free Crisp app for Mac and drag your clip onto the window. Nothing is uploaded: the whole job runs on your Mac.

  2. Open the Rotate tool

    In Crisp, select the clip on your timeline and find Rotate in the Tools panel.

  3. Choose a direction

    Pick 90° right or 90° left for a sideways clip, 180° for an upside-down one, Flip ↔ to mirror it, or Flip ↕ to turn it over.

  4. Press Apply

    Crisp rotates on-device with hardware encoding, keeps the audio, puts the result on your timeline and saves it beside your original.

Why the same clip looks sideways in only some apps

Phones often record upright but store the video sideways with a little rotation flag that says "turn me 90° on playback". Some players honour that flag; others ignore it, which is why the same file looks fine in Photos but sideways in a browser or on another device. Crisp doesn't rely on the flag: it bakes the rotation into the actual pixels and clears the flag, so the clip plays the right way up everywhere.

What each rotation does to the frame, and to the sound

Crisp offers five transforms, and the one thing worth knowing before you pick is which of them change the shape of the frame. A quarter turn swaps width and height; a half turn and the two mirrors leave them exactly as they were.

That matters more than it sounds: a 90° rotate turns a landscape clip into a genuine portrait file, which is what Reels, Shorts and TikTok want, and what a 16:9 timeline will letterbox. If you wanted the picture upright inside a landscape frame instead, rotate and then crop.

Your audio is not touched by any of them. A rotate re-encodes the picture (it has to, because the pixels genuinely move) but the sound is stream-copied straight across, so it comes out bit-identical and gains no generation loss however many times you rotate the file. The one exception is a source whose audio the output container cannot legally carry (ADPCM inside an .avi, WMA inside a .wmv) where Crisp re-encodes that track to AAC rather than fail the job. Ordinary iPhone, camera and screen-recording footage takes the copy path.

Rotation questions

How do I rotate a video on a Mac for free?

Use Crisp: drop your clip in, type "rotate it right" or "my video is sideways" in the box above the preview and press Do it, or pick a direction in the Rotate tool and press Apply. It rotates the clip and keeps the audio, entirely on your Mac, with no account, no upload.

How do I fix a sideways phone video?

A sideways clip just needs a 90° turn: type "my video is sideways" or pick Rotate right / Rotate left. Crisp turns it upright and bakes the rotation in, so it plays correctly in every player, not just one.

Can I flip it upside down or mirror it?

Yes. 180° Flips an upside-down clip, and Mirror flips it horizontally (handy for reversed front-camera footage).

Does rotating drop the audio or hurt quality?

No. The audio is kept, and Crisp hardware-encodes the picture at high quality on Apple Silicon, writing the file locally and atomically so you can check it immediately.

Can I rotate a photo?

Not in Crisp: rotating is for video clips, so the Rotate tool doesn't appear when a photo is selected.
