The short version

  1. Open Crisp

    Grab the free Crisp app for Mac and open it. Everything runs on your Mac. Your videos and photos never leave your computer.

  2. Drop the photo in

    Drag the photo anywhere onto the window, or press Import. It shows up in the Media panel as a photo card.

  3. Press Upscale photo

    Press Upscale photo on the photo's card. You can also select the photo, type upscale it to 4k in the prompt bar and press Do it. When the job finishes, Crisp says so.

  4. Find the copy

    The enlarged photo is saved next to the original, named yourphoto_crisp.jpg. The original is not touched.

How big the copy comes out

The Upscale photo button runs the Auto preset. Auto and the Enhance → 4K preset both aim at a picture about 2160 pixels tall and pick the closest whole-number factor: 2×, 3× or 4×. A photo around 720 pixels tall is enlarged 3×. A photo around 1080 pixels tall is enlarged 2×, which lands on exactly 3840×2160. A big phone photo is enlarged 2×, the smallest step, so it still gets the cleanup. Here is what the button did on four photos that are already on this site:

The prompt bar can pick a different factor from the button. Asked to upscale it to 4k, it enlarged the first photo 3× to 5112×2688, where the button chose 2×. If you care about the exact size, check the result.

The right-hand crops have steeper edges and quieter flat areas. They also look a little smoothed, in places almost painted. That is what drawn detail looks like up close. I would call it a clear gain for viewing and printing at a larger size, and not a recovery of what the camera missed.

iPhone photos work directly

An iPhone saves a photo as a HEIC file made of 512-pixel tiles. Version 1.0.1455 could not open them: its photo lane handed the file to its video tool with a filter, the tool opens a tiled HEIC its own way, and the two collided, so the job ended in an error in under a second and the photo's thumbnail stayed black. I found that while writing this page. Version 1.0.1458 reads the whole picture first, upright, and then works on it.

I checked it on the installed app with four files: a tiled HEIC, the same stored sideways with a rotation the way a portrait photo is, a 12-megapixel one at iPhone size (4032×3024), and a single-tile HEIC and a JPG as controls. All came out upright at the right size with a thumbnail in the media panel, and the 12-megapixel one took 17.4 seconds. The result is a lossless PNG next to the original, because Crisp cannot write HEIC. The files were written by my Mac's own converter, not taken from a phone's camera, so try one of yours and tell me if it misbehaves.

Does the free copy mark a photo?

No. I measured it on version 1.0.1455, and 1.0.1458 changed only how HEIC photos are read. The small “crispvideo.app” mark goes on video exports only. The code says photos are left alone, and I checked it instead of trusting the comment. I compared the free app's photo output with the engine's own output for the same photo: the largest difference was 27 brightness levels, scattered, with nothing in a corner. The control was a video run the same way, where about five thousand pixels in the bottom-right corner differed strongly. That is the mark, and it shows the check can see one. Policies change, so this describes this version.

What upscaling a photo can and cannot do

Upscaling draws what it expects to be there. That works well on edges, text-like shapes and noise, and it cannot rescue a photo that was blurred by focus or motion, because there is no sharp edge to find. I wrote that up in why AI upscaling can’t fix focus. If the photo is grainy, ask for it in one sentence, as how to fix a grainy photo on a Mac measures: a higher Denoise value removes more grain and costs some sharpness.

Two limits come from the code. A very large photo is capped: Crisp cannot write an intermediate image past about 268 megapixels, so a big photo is enlarged by a smaller factor, and if even 2× would be too big it tells you to resize the photo a little first. And Max quality, the most thorough restore, is a Pro feature that needs a one-time model pack of about 4.6 GB. Max works on a single photo on a Mac with 16 GB of memory. I did not measure it here.

Does it stay private? The photo never leaves your Mac. The offline editing page measured ten real jobs with no connection outside the machine.

Crisp 1.0.1455 on an M1 Ultra Mac Studio with other work running (load average 3 to 5), with a fresh profile so it ran as the free copy. Questions people ask

Can I upscale an iPhone HEIC photo in Crisp?

Yes, directly, from version 1.0.1458. Version 1.0.1455 failed on them with an error within a second, because a phone saves a HEIC as a grid of tiles and Crisp could not read one. Now it reads the whole picture first, upright. A 12-megapixel iPhone-size HEIC, stored rotated the way a portrait photo is, took 17.4 seconds on an M1 Ultra and came out as a 6048 by 8064 PNG, with its thumbnail in the media panel. I tested files my Mac wrote, not one straight from a phone's camera, so check yours.

Does Crisp put a watermark on an upscaled photo?

No. I measured it on version 1.0.1455, and version 1.0.1458 changed nothing about the mark. The free copy's small crispvideo.app mark goes on video exports only. I checked by comparing the free app's photo output with the engine's own output, which differed by at most 27 brightness levels with nothing clustered in a corner, while a video made the same way showed the mark in the bottom right.

How long does it take to upscale a photo?

On an M1 Ultra Mac Studio, 2.6 to 5.2 seconds for photos between 0.7 and 1.5 megapixels, enlarged to between 6 and 8 megapixels, with other work running on the Mac. Larger photos take longer. Treat that as a rough guide for your own Mac.

Does upscaling a photo add real detail?

It adds drawn detail. The model guesses what the edges and textures looked like, and a good guess looks sharp. In my crops edges came out steeper and flat areas cleaner, and some textures looked smoothed or painted. A photo that was never in focus has no real edge to recover, so it stays soft.
