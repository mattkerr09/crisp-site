The short version

  1. Open Crisp

    Grab the free Crisp app for Mac and open it. Everything runs on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded.

  2. Drop the photo in

    Drag the photo anywhere onto the window, or press Import. It shows up in the Media panel as a photo card.

How big the copy comes out

The Upscale photo button runs the Auto preset. Auto and the Enhance → 4K preset both aim at a picture about 2160 pixels tall and pick the closest whole-number factor: 2×, 3× or 4×. A photo around 720 pixels tall is enlarged 3×. A photo around 1080 pixels tall is enlarged 2×, which lands on exactly 3840×2160. A big phone photo is enlarged 2×, the smallest step, so it still gets the cleanup. Here is what the button did on four photos that are already on this site:

iPhone photos: one extra step

I found this while writing this page, so I will not skate over it. An iPhone saves a photo as a HEIC file made of 512-pixel tiles. Crisp's photo lane hands the file to its video tool with a filter, the tool opens a tiled HEIC its own way, and the two collide. The job ends in an error in under a second. A small HEIC with a single tile worked, and a JPG of the same picture worked. iPhone photos are normally stored as 512-pixel tiles, so I expect every one to fail. I tested a HEIC my Mac wrote, not a photo from a phone, so check yours, and plan on converting first.

The quickest fix is the one your Mac already has: select the photos in Finder, right-click, Quick Actions, Convert Image, pick JPEG at the highest quality, then drop the JPGs into Crisp. The details, including what a conversion keeps and drops, are in how to convert HEIC to JPG on a Mac. Keep the HEIC originals.

Does the free copy mark a photo?

In version 1.0.1455, no. The small “Made with Crisp” mark goes on video exports only. The code says photos are left alone, and I checked it instead of trusting the comment. I compared the free app's photo output with the engine's own output for the same photo: the largest difference was 27 brightness levels, scattered, with nothing in a corner. The control was a video run the same way, where about five thousand pixels in the bottom-right corner differed strongly. That is the mark, and it shows the check can see one. Policies change, so this describes this version.

What upscaling a photo can and cannot do

Upscaling draws what it expects to be there. That works well on edges, text-like shapes and noise, and it cannot rescue a photo that was blurred by focus or motion, because there is no sharp edge to find. I wrote that up in why AI upscaling can’t fix focus. If the photo is grainy, the Denoise slider decides how much grain the model removes, and a higher value costs some sharpness, which I measured in what a video enhancer does.

Two limits come from the code. A very large photo is capped: Crisp cannot write an intermediate image past about 268 megapixels, so a big photo is enlarged by a smaller factor, and if even 2× would be too big it tells you to resize the photo a little first. And Max quality, the most thorough restore, is a Pro feature that needs a one-time model pack of about 4.6 GB. Max works on a single photo on a Mac with 16 GB of memory. I did not measure it here.

Does it stay private? The photo never leaves your Mac. The offline editing page measured ten real jobs with no connection outside the machine.

Questions people ask

Can I upscale an iPhone HEIC photo in Crisp?

Not directly in version 1.0.1455. A HEIC from an iPhone is stored in tiles, and Crisp's photo lane fails on those files with an error within a second. Convert the photo to JPG first (Finder's Convert Image Quick Action takes a few seconds), then upscale the JPG. A small single-tile HEIC did work in my test, but iPhone photos are normally tiled. I tested a file my Mac wrote, not one from a phone.

Does Crisp put a watermark on an upscaled photo?

Not in version 1.0.1455. The free copy's small Made with Crisp mark goes on video exports only. I checked by comparing the free app's photo output with the engine's own output, which differed by at most 27 brightness levels with nothing clustered in a corner, while a video made the same way showed the mark in the bottom right.

How long does it take to upscale a photo?

On an M1 Ultra Mac Studio, 2.6 to 5.2 seconds for photos between 0.7 and 1.5 megapixels, enlarged to between 6 and 8 megapixels, with other work running on the Mac. Larger photos take longer. Treat that as a rough guide for your own Mac.

Does upscaling a photo add real detail?

It adds drawn detail. The model guesses what the edges and textures looked like, and a good guess looks sharp. In my crops edges came out steeper and flat areas cleaner, and some textures looked smoothed or painted. A photo that was never in focus has no real edge to recover, so it stays soft.
