The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and type it:

  • shrink the file size: compress it, balanced
  • make the file smaller: same thing, one tap
  • it's too big to email: compress for sending
  • compress it as small as possible, most aggressive

Press Do it and Crisp compresses the clip on your timeline and saves the smaller file beside your original.

Step by step

  1. Open Crisp and drop in your video

    Get the free Crisp app for Mac and drag your clip onto the window. Every step runs on your Mac; your videos never leave it.

  2. Ask for it

    Your clip is on the timeline, selected. Type “compress it to send” in the box above the preview.

  3. Say how much

    “Compress it a little” keeps the most quality, plain “compress it” is the balanced middle, and “compress it a lot” (or “as small as possible”) makes the smallest file. Crisp re-encodes at a fraction of the clip's own bitrate using HEVC. The dimensions stay the same.

  4. Press Do it

    The smaller .mp4 lands beside your original and on your timeline. If a clip is already small, Crisp tells you rather than making it bigger.

Smaller file, same resolution unless you name one, and never bigger

Compressing is not the same as resizing. Crisp keeps the pixels (a 1080p clip stays 1080p) and just lowers the bitrate, so the file shrinks while the picture looks the same at a glance. The three levels target roughly 30% / 50% / 70% of the clip's current bitrate, and because it measures the source, the output is genuinely smaller. An already-efficient clip is left alone, so “compress” never accidentally grows a file. Still too big for email? Name a smaller size (“make it 720p”) and Crisp brings the picture down to it and cuts the bitrate with it, or trim it shorter.

Compress vs resize (downscale)

Two different ways to make a video "smaller", pick by what you actually need:

  • Compress: shrink the file size, keep the resolution. For emailing/uploading a clip that's too big. That's this guide.
  • Resize / downscale: actually make the picture smaller (e.g. 4K → 1080p). Say “make it 1080p” (or 720p, 540p, 480p, 360p): Crisp brings the picture down to that size and shrinks the file with it. Or just ask to “lower the resolution”: with no size named, Crisp takes it one step down (1080p → 720p, 4K → 1080p) and says which on the plan before anything runs. Use Reframe for aspect changes.

Compression, in detail

How do I compress a video on a Mac for free?

Use Crisp: drop your clip in, select the clip and type it in the prompt bar (or type "shrink the file size"), choose a level, and press it. It shrinks the file on your Mac: no account, no upload.

Does it change the resolution?

Only if you ask. By default the dimensions stay the same: Crisp lowers the bitrate (HEVC), so the file is smaller but the picture looks the same. Name a smaller size (“make it 720p”) and it brings the picture down to that size too, which shrinks the file much further.

How small will it get?

“A lot” ≈ 30% of the clip's bitrate, plain “compress it” ≈ 50%, “a little” ≈ 70%, so roughly a third to two-thirds of the original on typical footage. Already-small clips are left alone.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. It's compressed entirely on your Mac, so there's no upload, no queue, no size cap.
