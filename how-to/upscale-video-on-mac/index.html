Upscaling 480p to 1080p

1080p is the honest target for a 480p source. From 640 by 480, a 1280 by 720 result means inventing about 3 pixels for every real one, 1920 by 1080 about 5 (the sweet spot), and 3840 by 2160 about 27, where the returns diminish and faces suffer. The failure at 4K is not that it looks blurry; it is that it looks confident and wrong: skin smooths into plastic, fabric weave becomes a repeating pattern the camera never saw, and small text becomes crisp nonsense. At 1080p the model has enough real signal to stay anchored, and most viewers are on a laptop or a phone, where 1080p is already at or beyond what the panel can show.

Do two things first. Denoise before upscaling: 480p sources are usually old and noisy, the model cannot tell noise from detail, and it will reconstruct the grain into permanent texture that no later denoise can remove. Deinterlace if it came off tape or a DVD, because comb teeth on moving edges get sharpened into permanent artefacts by any upscaler. Then go in one pass, not two. Do not go 480p to 720p today and 720p to 1080p next week: the second pass treats the first pass’s invented detail as real source material and compounds whatever it got wrong. Pick the final resolution and get there in a single operation.

Judge it properly. Watch it playing, at full size, not paused and not in a small preview. Frames are upscaled independently, so the model’s guesses differ slightly from frame to frame, and in flat areas (a wall, the sky, a plain shirt) that reads as a fine crawling shimmer that is invisible in a screenshot and obvious in motion. That is why still-frame comparisons of upscalers tell you almost nothing. Where the 480p came from matters. A file shot at 480p (a MiniDV camcorder, an early digital camera) holds close to 480 lines of real detail. A 480p file downscaled from something larger, or sent through a messaging app, has been through an encoder that threw detail away, yet it often improves more, because compression artefacts have consistent shapes a model has seen a million times. Check the frame rate. A lot of 480p material is 29.97 fps, not a round 30, a legacy of NTSC timing; treat it as 30 anywhere in the chain and the audio drifts out of sync by half a second over a long clip. Confirm the output rate matches the input.
