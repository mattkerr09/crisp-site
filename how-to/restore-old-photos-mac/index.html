Decide what needs another tool first

A scan can be grainy and damaged at the same time. Cleaning its texture will not mend a missing piece. Sort out those needs before running a folder, so a smoother surface does not get mistaken for a repaired photograph.

Crisp cannot remove scratches or tears, colourise photos, restore faces, or automatically repair faded colour. Those need another tool. Dust removal is a video option only.

For a face enhancement option, MyHeritage Photo Enhancer upscales faces in a photo through a web upload and requires free signup. No price was read. That is a choice if you want face enhancement and accept uploading the picture, not evidence that it will mend physical damage.

Checked on MyHeritage Photo Enhancer's own page, 20 November 2026.

If your aim is to preserve what was recorded, keep the scan alongside every edited copy. Crisp's panel puts its approach plainly: "Faces are cleaned like everything else, never redrawn." It does not offer face restoration.

Restore a folder of old photos in Crisp

  1. Gather pictures that need the same treatment

    Put the photos you want to work on in a folder. The Old photos panel uses one look for the whole batch. A group of small scans for printing may need a different choice from pictures you only want cleaned.

  2. Open Old photos and give it the folder

    In Crisp on your Mac, open the Old photos panel and choose the whole folder. This is the folder route. For one enlarged picture instead, the Upscale photo button on a photo's card saves its copy next to the original.

  3. Pick a single look for the batch

    Choose Clean to denoise and sharpen. Choose Enlarge 2x for enlargement, or Enlarge 4x for printing. Sepia gives the batch a sepia look. It is a style choice, not colourising or automatic faded-colour repair.

  4. Choose whether to save comparisons

    The panel can also save a before-and-after image of each photo. Use that option if you want to review the batch or show someone how a copy differs from the scan. A comparison helps you notice a change without losing sight of the source.

  5. Run the batch and inspect the new copies

    New copies go in a Crisp restored folder. Originals never change. Open a result beside the original and check small features as well as the whole picture before sending it to print or sharing it.

Review the parts that matter

  • Faces: look for a useful cleanup while keeping the person recognisable. A smoother cheek is not proof of recovered facial detail.
  • Clothes and hair: compare fine texture. If the cleaned version loses something you value, keep the original scan available too.
  • Borders and damage: scratches and missing areas require a different repair tool; compare them in both images.
  • The whole batch: check both light and dark pictures. One look can suit some scans better than others, so split the folder when needed.

Crisp runs offline. Your videos and photos never leave your computer. Photo processing is Mac only, even though the product download box also offers Windows for video work.

What the published grain test tells us

The grain walkthrough published 11 October 2026 used installed Crisp 1.0.1460 on an M1 Ultra Mac Studio. Grain was added to known clean pictures. The request set Denoise 0.6 and landed 0.4 to 2.9 dB closer to the clean original than the Upscale photo button, as the measured results show.

That result supports trying grain cleanup. It does not measure the Old photos panel, scan damage, colour fading or the quality of a print. There is no before-and-after benchmark for this folder panel on this page. Review your own batch before deciding the result is good enough.

If you only need a larger single image, see the image upscaler choices. If the problem is still unclear, the photo enhancer list separates enlargement, grain and face enhancement.

Questions about restoring an album

Where will the Old photos panel put the finished pictures?

It saves new copies in a Crisp restored folder. It never changes the originals. This differs from the single-photo Upscale photo button, which saves the enlarged copy next to its source.

Can each photo have a different look in the same batch?

The Old photos panel uses one look for the batch. Put pictures that need Clean in one folder and those that need a different enlargement or Sepia in another, then process each group with its chosen look.

Does the Sepia choice bring back a print's lost colours?

No. Sepia is a look you choose for the batch. Crisp cannot colourise photos or automatically repair faded colour. Use another tool for those changes.

Can I save a comparison for every restored photo?

Yes. The Old photos panel can save a before-and-after image of each photo as well as the new copy. Review it against the original scan before sharing the result.
