The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and type what you want:

  • mute it, or remove the audio, silence the clip, get rid of the sound, cut the audio, turn off the sound
  • make it louder, or boost the volume, turn it up, too quiet, twice as loud
  • make it quieter, or lower the volume, turn it down, too loud, half as loud

Press Do it and Crisp changes the clip on your timeline. A "mute"-type phrase drops the audio entirely; a "louder"-type phrase sets the volume to 2× (louder); a "quieter"-type phrase sets it to 0.5× (quieter). You can set an exact level by typing it: “make it 3 times louder” or “volume 0.5x”.

It's careful about intent, too. "mute" and "silent" are matched as whole words on purpose, so "make it muted" (a muted, desaturated color look) stays a color grade, not an audio change, and "the dancer moves silently" is read as a description of the shot, not a command. And if you ask for a bigger edit in the same breath, "mute it and upscale to 4K", Crisp splits the request and plans both as two steps, so nothing gets dropped.

The manual way: the Audio tool

  1. Open Crisp and drop in your video

    Get the free Crisp app for Mac and drag your clip onto the window. Every step runs on your Mac; your videos never leave it.

  2. Open the Audio tool

    In Crisp, select the clip on your timeline and find Audio in the Tools panel. It's for video clips. And if the clip has no audio track at all, Crisp tells you there's nothing to mute rather than silently doing nothing.

  3. Choose Mute or Volume

    Pick Mute, Louder or Quieter in its menu. Mute removes the audio track entirely. Louder sets 1.6× and Quieter sets 0.55×. Typed requests are stronger: “make it louder” sets 2×, roughly twice as loud, and “make it quieter” sets 0.5×, about half as loud. For an exact level, type it (“make it 3 times louder”, “volume 0.5x”) anywhere from 0.1× to 4.0×, or step the clip's Volume menu under This clip through 0.25×, 0.5×, 1×, 1.5×, 2×, 3× or 4×. That menu and its Mute this clip box are live settings, applied when you export.

  4. Press Apply

    With Pro, Crisp copies your video stream byte-for-byte and saves the result beside your original. The free version runs the whole clip and adds its small “crispvideo.app” mark, which re-encodes the picture once. On mute it drops the audio; on a volume change it re-encodes only the audio at the new level.

Why muting with Crisp Pro is instant and lossless

Most editors re-export the whole file to change the sound, which re-compresses your video and softens it a little every time. With Pro, Crisp doesn't touch the picture: it stream-copies the video byte-for-byte and only removes (mute) or re-encodes (volume) the audio. So a mute is essentially a fast remux with no quality loss. The exact same frames, just without the sound. A volume change re-encodes only the audio to high-bitrate AAC, leaving the video identical. It also keeps your source container where it can: an .mp4, .mov or .mkv stays as-is, and only falls back to .mkv for formats (like webm or avi) that can't cleanly carry the copied video, so the export never fails on a container mismatch. The free version adds its “crispvideo.app” mark, which re-encodes the picture once.

Two things that go wrong with audio elsewhere, and what Crisp does instead

A screen recording usually has more than one audio track. OBS and ScreenFlow write your mic and the system audio as separate tracks; a camera or a download may carry English and French. Plenty of tools quietly keep only the first one, so "make it louder" hands back a file that lost half its sound. Mute in Crisp drops every track, which is what muting means, and every other audio effect processes every track and keeps them all. Verified on a two-track file: both tracks present afterwards, and both moved from −21.1 dB to −27.2 dB under a volume change.

A track can exist and carry no sound at all: a screen recording made with no mic, a drone clip, an export that was already muted. Ask to normalise that and the honest answer is that there is nothing to normalise, but the usual behaviour is to re-encode the whole video for several minutes and give you back the same silence with a tick next to it. Crisp measures the track first and tells you. The test is the loudest sample, not the average: a genuinely silent track floors at about −91 dB, while a −45 dB whisper (exactly the audio someone most wants lifted) still peaks around −62.7 dB, so an average would refuse the very clips it should accept. Measuring costs 0.16 seconds on a five-minute 720p clip, against a re-encode of the entire file.

Muting itself skips all of that with Pro: the picture is copied rather than re-encoded, so it finishes at copy speed on a clip of any length, and the video comes out byte-for-byte identical. The free version re-encodes it once to add the mark.

What mute and volume are, and aren't

Mute and volume do two things cleanly: mute a clip, or scale its whole volume up or down. That covers the overwhelming majority of "mute video" and "make my video louder" searches: the wind-noise kill, the too-quiet phone clip, the silent meme.

What they deliberately don't try to be is a full audio mixer: there's no ducking (auto-lowering music under a voice) and no multi-track mixing. Two neighbours cover the rest: a fade brings the sound up and down with the picture, and the Project panel's Music menu puts a song under your edit. For frame-precise audio automation you'd want a timeline app, we'd rather tell you that than pretend. For a clean mute or a whole-clip volume change, though, Crisp is the fastest, most private way to do it on a Mac.

Muting questions

How do I mute a video on a Mac for free?

Use Crisp: drop your clip in, select the clip and use Audio in the Tools panel and choose Mute (or type "mute it"), then export. It drops the audio track entirely on your Mac, with no account and no upload. With Pro it copies the video byte-for-byte, fast and lossless. The free version adds its “crispvideo.app” mark, which re-encodes the picture once.

Will muting lower the quality of my video?

With Pro, no. The free version adds its “crispvideo.app” mark, which re-encodes the picture once. Muting only removes the audio and, with Pro, the video stream is copied byte-for-byte, so the picture is not re-encoded and there's no quality loss. That's also why it's near-instant.

How do I make a video louder or quieter instead?

Pick Louder or Quieter in the Audio tool, or type an exact level anywhere from 0.1× to 4.0×, such as “make it 3 times louder”. Around 2× is roughly twice as loud, 0.5× about half. With Pro the video is still copied untouched and only the audio is re-encoded at the new level.

Can I mute a photo?

No: a still image has no sound, so the Audio tool is for video clips only.

Does Crisp do fades, ducking, or add music to a clip?

Fades and music, yes; ducking, no. A fade brings the sound in and out with the picture, and the Project panel's Music menu lays a song under your edit. Mute and volume themselves are whole-clip changes, with no ducking or multi-track mixing.
