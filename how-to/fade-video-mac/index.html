How to fade a video in and out on a Mac

You shouldn't need a timeline editor, an account, or an upload for that. Here's how to fade a video on a Mac in seconds, 100% offline, with the audio fading right along with the picture: either by picking it in the Project panel or by typing "fade it in and out".

The fastest way: just ask

Crisp has a plain-English box above the preview (“Tell Crisp what to do with the selected clip…”). Drop your video in and type what you want:

  • fade it in and out: the classic top-and-tail
  • fade in (or fade in from black)
  • fade out (or fade out to black)
  • add a fade, or fade in/out

Press Do it and Crisp fades the clip on your timeline. It's careful about which words are a real command: "fade it in", "fade this out", "fade the whole thing out" all route to a fade, but a phrase like a faded look is a colour treatment (a desaturated, washed-out grade), not a black fade, so Crisp grades those instead. And if you ask for a bigger edit in the same breath, "fade it in and upscale to 4K", Crisp splits the request and plans both as two steps, so nothing gets dropped.

The manual way: the Project panel's Fade menu

  1. Open Crisp and drop in your video

    Get the free Crisp app for Mac and drag your clip onto the window. Every step runs on your Mac; your videos never leave it.

  2. Open the Project panel

    Press ‹ Back to project above the clip's settings. The Project panel's fades apply to the start and end of your whole edit; with one clip on the timeline, that's the clip.

  3. Choose In, Out or Both

    Pick In, Out or Both in the Fade menu. In ramps up from black at the start, Out ramps down to black at the end, and Both does the two. The menu uses a natural one-second fade; for another length, type it: “fade in over 2 seconds”.

  4. Export

    Crisp fades the picture and the audio together when you press Export video, on-device with hardware encoding, and saves the result beside your original, ready to post. A typed fade renders on the clip straight away instead.

Short clips are handled for you

On a very short clip, a one-second fade in and a one-second fade out could overlap, or swallow the whole thing. Crisp clamps the fade length automatically, so each side is limited to a fraction of the clip's length. You always get a clean, non-overlapping fade, whether the clip is ten seconds or one. And the fade-out is anchored to the audio's own end, so if your sound runs a touch longer than the video (a music bed, a voiceover tail), it still fades out properly instead of cutting off early.

Exactly how long a fade you get

"Clamped to a fraction of the clip" is the honest summary; here is the actual arithmetic, because it is the difference between the number you type and the number you see. A fade is allowed to be 0.1 to 10 seconds. On top of that, an in-and-out pair is capped at 45% of the clip per side (so the two can never meet in the middle), and a single-ended fade at 90%.

What Crisp's Fade is, and isn't

Crisp's fade does one thing cleanly: it fades your clip up from black, down to black, or both, with the audio fading in step and hardware encoding on your Mac. That covers the overwhelming majority of "fade video" searches: the tidy intro, the graceful ending, the top-and-tail before you post.

What it deliberately doesn't try to be is a timeline NLE. It fades to and from black (not an arbitrary colour), it fades the ends of the clip (not a fade in the middle), and it's a single-clip effect: a crossfade that dissolves one clip into another is a different job (Crisp does clip-to-clip transitions in its Montage feature, not here). If you need a mid-clip dip-to-black with keyframes inside a longer edit, that's a job for Premiere or Final Cut. For a clean fade in/out on a clip, though, Crisp is the fastest, most private way to do it on a Mac.

Fades, in detail

How do I fade a video in and out on a Mac for free?

Use Crisp: drop your clip in, type "fade it in and out" in the box above the preview and press Do it, or pick a Fade in the Project panel and export. It fades the picture and the audio together, entirely on your Mac, with no account, no upload.

Does the audio fade too?

Yes. Crisp fades the sound in step with the video, and the fade-out is anchored to the audio's own end, so a music bed or voiceover tail that runs slightly past the video still fades out cleanly instead of cutting off early.

Can I fade to white instead of black?

Not yet: Crisp's fade goes to and from black. Fading to an arbitrary colour is a timeline-editor feature Crisp doesn't try to replicate.

What about a crossfade between two clips?

That's a different job. A fade in/out tops-and-tails a single clip; dissolving one clip into another is a transition: pick Dissolve (or Wipe, Slide or Circle) in the Project panel's Transition menu and every cut in your edit gets it.

Can I fade a photo?

Not on its own: a photo has no running time to fade over. Put it on the timeline, where it becomes a still clip, and the Project panel's Fade fades your edit in and out, photo included.
