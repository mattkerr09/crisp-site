More than an upscaler

Old film, cleaned up

Dust and blotches that flash for a single frame get voted away by comparing neighbouring frames: nothing is invented, and marks that genuinely persist are left alone. Old footage that pulses in brightness gets steadied too, while deliberate fades are kept. Scratches run across many frames, so they are outside what this does and we don't pretend otherwise.

FAQ

Clear answers.

Is it really 100% offline?

Yes. Crisp runs entirely on your Mac. No upload, no account, no queue, and it works with the Wi-Fi switched off. The only things that ever touch the network are checking for an update, activating a licence, and downloading the optional Max model pack, never your footage.

How is this different from Topaz?

Topaz moved to a subscription, and its Starlight restore models need either macOS 26 or 36 GB of memory depending on the variant. Crisp is a one-time-owned, fully-offline app with a generative-restore lane on-device, an AUTO mode instead of a 15-model picker, a low-light stage Topaz lacks, and interpolation that keeps your audio.

Do I need a powerful Mac?

Any Apple-Silicon Mac runs the fast tier (1080p → 4K at interactive speed). The generative Max tier is an optional 4.6 GB download, wants 32 GB+ of memory, and is far slower than the word “overnight” suggests. At 1080p output on a fast desktop Mac it takes at least about 1.8 hours for five seconds of video, 11 hours for thirty seconds, and 43 hours for two minutes. Crisp shows you that estimate before the job starts, not three minutes into it. Treat Max as a seconds-at-a-time tool for the one shot that matters — Enhance is the lane for whole clips.

Will it strip my audio or drift out of sync?

No. Audio is stream-copied and never re-timed, even when you interpolate to a higher frame rate. Color is converted and re-tagged correctly, so nothing comes out washed-out.

What about damaged, dark, or noisy footage?

That's exactly the target. Crisp denoises, lifts shadows, and restores detail before it upscales, so low-light and compressed clips come out clean instead of amplifying the mess.

Does it do photos and editing too?

Yes. Drop a JPEG, PNG, or HEIC and Crisp upscales & restores the still in about a second. And the built-in Montage mode auto-edits a long video into a highlight reel, a tightened condense, or a music montage cut to the beat — all on-device, no timeline required.

What does it cost?

No subscription, ever. The Crisp editor and 4K upscaler are free (exports carry a small “Made with Crisp” mark). Crisp Pro is a one-time $19 — it removes the watermark and unlocks Max Quality + batch. No monthly bill, no credits, no cloud.
