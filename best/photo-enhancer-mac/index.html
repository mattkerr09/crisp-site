Best picks for the change you want

The grain result, and what it actually proves

The grain guide published 11 October 2026 measured Crisp 1.0.1460 on an M1 Ultra Mac Studio. Grain was added to photos with a known clean original, so it was possible to measure how close each output came to that original. This is a controlled grain test, not a comparison with the other apps above.

To try that route, add the photo to the timeline, type fix the grain in this photo and press Do it. The request used Denoise 0.6 and landed 0.4 to 2.9 dB closer to the clean original than the Upscale photo button. See the full measurement and crops beside that figure. The default button could still look smoother to the eye, especially with heavy grain.

A clean original is rarely available for a real family picture. Look at your result, not just a score from another photo. If skin loses its texture or fine lines merge, keep the original and compare a less changed copy before deciding what to share.

Match the fix to the fault

Small picture, enough detail

Read the image upscaler list. More pixels and cleaner texture are separate goals. Judge the enlarged output at its intended viewing size.

Speckled flat areas

Try the grain request on a copy. Compare smooth backgrounds and fine texture in the same picture; removing one can also remove the other.

A folder of scans

Use the old photo restoration steps to pick one look for each batch. Separate pictures that need different treatment before running the folder.

Physical damage or missing colour

Crisp cannot remove scratches or tears, colourise photos, restore faces, or automatically repair faded colour. Those need another tool. Dust removal is a video option only.

What to keep private and what to keep unchanged

Your videos and photos never leave your computer. Crisp runs offline on a Mac for photo work. The Windows download is for video upscaling, not photo enhancement. A web upload is a different choice; decide whether you want to send the picture before opening a web tool.

Keep a separate copy of the source. An enhanced picture is an edited version of the image you started with. Compare it with that source before replacing anything in a family album, archive or print order.

Photo enhancer questions

Is enlarging a picture the same as removing its grain?

No. Enlargement changes the output size. Grain cleanup changes texture. Crisp's measured grain request used Denoise 0.6 and gave a different result from its Upscale photo button. Choose the change your picture needs.

Can I treat a photo enhancer score as a promise for my album?

No. A score against a known original says how that test behaved. Your album has different faces, grain and lighting, and probably no clean reference. Compare a copy by eye before processing more pictures.

Which of these choices asks me to upload a picture?

Picsart and MyHeritage Photo Enhancer use web uploads. Crisp's photo tools run offline on your Mac. Choose whether you want to upload before choosing a tool.

Should I use the same fix for grain and a faded print?

No. Crisp can reduce grain, but it cannot automatically repair faded colour. That needs another tool. Keep the scan so any later colour work starts with the source.
