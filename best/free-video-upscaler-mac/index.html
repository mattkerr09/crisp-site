What the free command-line route costs, measured

Real-ESRGAN upscales images (its README, read 11 November 2026), so free AI upscaling of a video means turning the video into images first. We measured what that means for disk space by upscaling real 1080p frames 2× with a Real-ESRGAN-family model. A 1080p frame saved as PNG was 1.52 MB on average. The same frame upscaled to 4K was 8.42 MB.

One minute of 1080p at 30 fps, upscaled to 4KScratch space needed
Source frames as PNG (1,800 frames)2.7 GB
Upscaled frames as PNG15.2 GB
Total before you re-encode17.9 GB

Then comes the time: a fast 2× model took 0.72 to 0.82 seconds a 4K frame on a Mac Studio, about 23 minutes for that one minute, and heavier models take longer. After that you still re-encode the frames into a video and put the audio back, and each of those is a command you have to get right.

Crisp does the same work in 20-second segments and deletes each one's frames before starting the next. Worked out from the same frame sizes, the frames it holds on disk peak at about 7 GB for a 1080p-to-4K job, however long the clip is; only the finished video grows with its length. That figure is calculated from how the app is built, not measured on a long render.

The free option nobody mentions

If your footage is sharp and you only need bigger pixel dimensions, for a 4K timeline or a delivery spec, a plain resize is free, fast and the most faithful choice:

ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf scale=3840:-2:flags=lanczos -c:a copy out.mp4

It won't add detail, but it won't invent any either. On real frames we halved and enlarged back, a Lanczos resize matched the original's pixels most closely on three of four frames, and kept colour best on all four. Free upscaling questions

Is there a free AI video upscaler for Mac?

Yes, if you are comfortable with a command line. Real-ESRGAN has a free macOS build (its README, read 11 November 2026), but it works on images, so you split the video into frames, upscale them and re-encode. For one minute of 1080p going to 4K we measured about 17.9 GB of frames on disk. In an app, Crisp's free tier upscales video with a small mark, up to 15 seconds per export.

Does Upscayl upscale video?

No. Upscayl's own README describes it as a tool to "enlarge and enhance low-resolution images" (read 11 November 2026). It is free and open source, and its README gives macOS 12 as the minimum.

Does Video2X work on a Mac?

Not natively. Its README lists Windows and Linux builds, offers a container image (Docker or Podman) for macOS, and asks for a Vulkan GPU and an Intel or AMD processor with AVX2 (read 11 November 2026).

Is a plain resize good enough?

For sharp footage going to a bigger screen, often yes. A Lanczos resize is free, fast and adds no detail that wasn't there. AI upscaling earns its time on low-resolution, compressed or noisy footage.
