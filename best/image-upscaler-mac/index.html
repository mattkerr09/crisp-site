How this comparison works

Our criteria are the price to own or use a tool for one year, free access and its limit, support for Mac, whether the image stays on the Mac or is uploaded, and enlargement features. Each vendor's own page was read on 20 November 2026. Free and open-source choices lead, including ongoing free tiers, in alphabetical order. Paid tools come next by their lowest stated year of use or purchase cost, ties alphabetically. Trials do not count as free; unknown yearly costs come last. This is a comparison of stated facts, not a test score. Crisp sits where that order puts it.

The table includes free limits even when they do not cover a whole film or full-size image. Listed offers are those shown when read; check the linked plan before paying. Unknown details stay unknown.

Crisp: enlargement alongside video work

Drop a photo into Crisp and press Upscale photo on its card. The new copy appears beside the original. Your videos and photos never leave your computer. Mac photo upscales are unmarked and work one photo at a time in the free version; Pro adds batch processing.

The photo guide published 10 October 2026 also reports that a 12-megapixel HEIC took 17 seconds with Crisp 1.0.1458 on an M1 Ultra Mac Studio. These are our measurements from those builds. They do not time the Old photos panel or establish how any rival performs.

Crisp cannot remove scratches or tears, colourise photos, restore faces, or automatically repair faded colour. Those need another tool. Dust removal is a video option only.

Lightroom Classic and Camera Raw: double the dimensions

Super Resolution doubles width and height and therefore gives four times the original pixels. It cannot be applied above 128 megapixels or when either dimension exceeds 32,500 pixels. Those are input limits, not proof that every enlarged detail is accurate.

Changing dimensions without an AI model

For a layout that needs a different pixel size, Apple Preview's Tools > Adjust Size offers a resampling control. Its guide describes changing dimensions, not AI detail recovery. We list it here as context rather than an AI upscaler with an unverified factor.

Checked on Apple Preview's own page, 20 November 2026.

Choose a target based on the print or display. Compare lettering, hair and repeating lines at the same displayed size. Larger dimensions alone cannot establish whether a texture was present in the source.

Questions about this comparison

Which image routes keep an enlargement on my Mac?

The processing column distinguishes local tools from uploads and unknown locations. Crisp handles photos locally on Mac, and its photo tools are not in the Windows app.

Do the four photo timings measure every enlargement tool?

No. They describe the named Crisp build on four sample photos. A different file, Mac or app needs its own timing.

Should I choose the largest enlargement factor available?

Choose the size you need to view or print. Inspect that result before enlarging it again. Extra pixels do not prove that fine detail matches the original.

What should I compare before keeping an AI enlarged copy?

Check the source and output at the same displayed size. Look at small text, hair and repeating patterns, then keep the untouched source with the copy.
