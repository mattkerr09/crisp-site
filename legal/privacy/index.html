Privacy Policy

Crisp · built in Grand Rapids · published 2026-07-28 · last updated 2026-11-11
Quick answer

Crisp processes your photos and videos entirely on your own computer, Mac or PC. They are never uploaded. Beyond licences and updates, the app sends four anonymous counts, once each, and nothing else about how you use it.

Crisp is published by Kerr & Company LLC, a Michigan limited liability company, which is the data controller for any personal information described below.

Last updated: 11 November 2026. This page describes what the software actually does (it was written against the shipping code, not from a template) and, since 13 August, what this website does too.

Your media never leaves your device

Every operation (upscaling, low-light repair, montages, split screen, exports) runs locally using models bundled with the app. Crisp has no upload endpoint and no server that receives your files. You can verify this by running Crisp with networking disabled: everything except licence activation and update checks continues to work.

No analytics in the app, and four anonymous counts

Crisp contains no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no advertising identifiers, and no third-party scripts. Nothing reports which features you use, which files you open, or how often you launch the app.

Since its updates of November 2026 it sends four anonymous counts, each at most once per computer: that the app opened for the first time, that a free copy finished its first export, that a free export first reached the free limit, and your one-tap answer to “How did you find Crisp?” (if you press Skip, nothing is sent). Each count is the event and, for the question, one of eight fixed answers. We keep a daily total of each and nothing else: no name, email, address, file, file name or identifier. Your computer remembers that it already sent them, so it never sends them twice.

The website is a separate thing, and it counts visits

Everything above is about the software. This site, crispvideo.app, is not the software, and it is honest to say what it does separately.

  • Page views, counted without a cookie. Every page on this site loads plausible.io/js/script.js, which records that a page was viewed. The URL, the referrer, and coarse details like browser and country. It sets no cookie, does not follow you between sites, and does not build a profile. It is how we know whether anyone read the thing we just wrote. If you would rather not be counted, any content blocker stops it, and nothing on the site depends on it loading.
  • No advertising pixel. Until 6 November 2026 every page here also loaded Meta’s advertising pixel, a script loaded from Facebook’s servers, which set a cookie named _fbp and told Meta that a page was viewed. It has been removed. This site no longer contacts Facebook or Meta and sets no advertising cookie; the cookieless page counter above is the only analytics left. The app never had it.
  • Purchases and Meta, until 7 November 2026. Changed 7 November 2026: from 24 August to 7 November 2026, when someone bought Crisp, our sales system sent Meta (Facebook) the price, the currency and a one-way coded copy of the buyer’s email address (a SHA-256 hash), so Meta could count the sale for ads. We switched it off on 7 November. Nothing about a purchase goes to Meta now.
  • No affiliate cookie. Until 2 November 2026 the homepage loaded a small script, /sled.js, that kept an affiliate referral code in a cookie named ta_ref and told usesled.com when someone arrived on a referral link. It has been removed: this site no longer sets that cookie and never contacts usesled.com.
  • The amount you paid, on the thank-you page only. After a successful purchase, the thank-you page asks our own server (kerr-affiliate-hub.kerrco.workers.dev) how much that payment was, sending only the payment’s id, so the sale is counted in our analytics at the price you actually paid rather than the list price. Nothing about you is sent or returned (the answer is the amount, the currency and the product) and the licence key and email on the address you were returned to are removed from it before any analytics script loads.
  • Affiliate referrals, on every page. If you arrive from an affiliate or partner link, the link’s referral code is kept in your browser’s local storage on this site (key kc_aff) for 30 days and is attached to the checkout link if you buy, so the partner who referred you is credited. This is first-party: it is stored only by this site, is not a cookie, is not sent to any third party by this site, and is not read by any other site. Clearing your browser’s site data removes it. The script that does this is snippet.js, loaded from kerr-affiliate-hub.kerrco.workers.dev; fetching it is the only request it makes, and it sets no cookie and sends nothing anywhere. Dodo Payments, our merchant of record named further down this page, receives the code as order metadata when a purchase is made.

    Worth stating plainly, because this page has been wrong before by omission: this is now the only affiliate mechanism on the site (the ta_ref cookie above is gone), it is local storage on every page, kept for 30 days, and a referral code is a tracker in the plain sense of the word (it records where you came from so someone can be paid), so nothing on this site says we do not track you.
  • Privacy-friendly site analytics. This page said “no site analytics … there is no Plausible” until 17 August 2026, and that was wrong: Plausible has been on all 114 pages of this site. It is corrected here rather than quietly deleted, because a privacy policy that has been inaccurate should say so. Plausible is cookieless, stores no IP addresses, does not track you across sites, and counts pageviews only. It is what tells us which comparison pages people read. The homepage also carried a second, duplicate Plausible install until the same date, so its visits were being counted twice; that one is gone.
  • Self-hosted assets. The page loads no external fonts, stylesheets or images. The two other domains it links to (github.com for the download and topazlabs.com in a comparison) are ordinary links you have to click.

None of this touches your media. Nothing you open in Crisp is ever seen by this website, because the website and the app do not talk to each other.

If you ask us to email you

The home page has two optional email fields, and each is its own list: the one in the footer, for news of Crisp, and the older-Mac one beside the macOS requirement, for the day Crisp runs on a Mac older than that. If you put your address in one and press its button, we store three things: the address, which site and list it came from, and when. Nothing else: no IP address, no browser, no referring page, no profile. It is our own database, not a marketing service, and nothing is sent anywhere when the page merely loads: the address travels once, when you press the button.

The footer list hears when a new restore lane or model improvement ships. The older-Mac list hears about that one thing only (the day it works) and nothing else. A third list, for Windows, had its own box on the home page until 5 November 2026, when Crisp for Windows shipped; it holds no addresses. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and asking us to delete the address removes the row.

The free video helper in ChatGPT and Claude

Separate from the app and this site, we run a free advice service at mcp.crispvideo.app that ChatGPT or Claude can use when you add it as an app or connector. It answers questions about old tapes, faults in old video, camcorder files and sending video, from tables built into it. It never receives a video, never opens or sends a file, and runs no command: the ffmpeg commands it writes are text for you to run yourself, if you choose to.

  • What it receives: only what the assistant sends it as a question's inputs, for example a cassette label, a tape format and how many tapes, symptoms in your words, a destination such as Gmail, a length, or a file name you mention. Never your conversation, your account, or anything on your computer.
  • What it is for: working out the answer and sending it back. Nothing else.
  • What it keeps: nothing it is sent. It writes no log of inputs. It keeps one number, how many questions it answered today, so it can stop at its daily limit, and a short-lived counter per session so one conversation cannot flood it.
  • Who else is involved: Cloudflare hosts it and keeps its own short-lived traffic and security records under Cloudflare's privacy policy. Nobody else receives anything. Inputs are never sold or shared, and never used for advertising or training. ChatGPT and Claude handle your conversation under OpenAI's and Anthropic's own policies.
  • Your controls: there is nothing to delete. To stop using it, remove the app or connector in ChatGPT or Claude. Questions go to support@crispvideo.app.

The only times Crisp contacts the internet

There are exactly six, and each is listed with what is sent:

  • Licence activation and validation (live.dodopayments.com, or api.polar.sh for licences bought before 20 August 2026), when you activate Crisp Pro, or when a Pro copy re-checks its licence (about once a week; the check never uses up an activation). Sent: your licence key, our product or organisation identifier, and a device label, which is this computer's name (cut to 64 characters). Note that computer names, on a Mac or a PC, often contain a person's name (for example "Matt's MacBook Pro"); that label is stored by whichever of the two issued your licence, so you can see and manage which machines a licence is active on.
  • Update checks (crispvideo.app): the app periodically fetches a small file listing the latest version (updater.json on a Mac, updater-windows.json on a PC). Sent: nothing beyond the ordinary web request itself (your IP address and user agent, as with visiting any website).
  • Downloading an update (github.com): only when you accept an update.
  • Downloading the optional Max pack, on a Mac only (Max quality does not run on Windows), and only if you choose to install it. It comes from huggingface.co (the models), github.com and codeload.github.com (a Python runtime and the SeedVR2 source) and PyPI, pypi.org and files.pythonhosted.org (the Python packages it needs). Sent: nothing about you or your files, beyond the ordinary web request each of those sees.
  • The founding-price count (kerr-lead-agent.kerrco.workers.dev), on a copy that isn’t Pro, when you open the unlock window or the app is about to quote the Pro price: how many founding-price places are left, so the price you see is the one checkout charges. Sent: nothing beyond the ordinary web request itself. We keep a daily total of these requests, with no address or identifier.
  • The four anonymous counts (kerr-lead-agent.kerrco.workers.dev), once each per computer, as described above: the app’s first opening, a free copy’s first finished export, a free export first reaching the free limit, and your answer to “How did you find Crisp?”. Sent: the event and the fixed answer, with the ordinary web request itself. We keep daily totals, with no address or identifier.

One more, and it is the installer, not the app: on Windows, if the PC does not already have Microsoft's WebView2 component, the installer downloads it from Microsoft while it installs. Nothing about you or your files is sent beyond the ordinary web request.

If you never activate a licence and decline updates, Crisp makes no outbound connections at all beyond the update check, the founding-price count and the two one-time counts, which you can block at the firewall without affecting editing.

What is stored on your computer

Your licence key and activation record are stored in Crisp's application-support folder (Library/Application Support/Crisp in your home folder on a Mac, the Crisp folder in %APPDATA% on a PC) so you do not have to re-enter them. Your projects, media paths and preferences stay on your computer. A small file there, counts.json, remembers which of the four anonymous counts were already sent. Deactivating your licence in the app removes the stored licence record.

Payments

Purchases are handled by a merchant of record, and Crisp never sees or stores your card details either way. Since 20 August 2026 that merchant is Dodo Payments, whose handling of your payment and contact information is governed by Dodo's privacy policy. Purchases made before that date went through Polar and are governed by Polar's privacy policy; those licences still work, so Polar may still hold the record of your purchase.

Your rights

Because Crisp holds no account and no server-side profile of you, there is very little data to exercise rights over. For anything held by our merchant of record in connection with your purchase (your email address, licence key and activation labels), you may request access, correction or deletion by contacting us at support@crispvideo.app, and we will action it with Dodo Payments, or with Polar if you bought before 20 August 2026. Deleting a licence record ends your ability to use Pro features on that machine.

Children

Crisp is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
