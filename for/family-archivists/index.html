Get the transfer right first

The capture sets the limit. Save a lossless or lightly compressed master before you do any restoration. Work from that master each time, rather than from an earlier compressed export. Keep it even if you like the restored version.

Check interlaced motion before judging sharpness

Interlaced tape holds two moments in each frame. Look for comb-shaped stripes along moving edges. Use Keep tape motion for interlaced tape, then check the export while it plays. Enlarging those stripes only makes them easier to see. Read the deinterlacing guide.

What AI can and cannot recover

AI can help with mild softness, tape noise, colour speckle and compression damage. It cannot recover a face that was only a few pixels wide, unreadable text or detail lost to a damaged tape. A sharp result can still be wrong. Keep the source and compare.

Be conservative with faces

Compare faces before and after at full size, then watch them move. If skin looks waxy or a face changes, prefer the gentler result.

Here is what Crisp does to a face. It has no face model, and nothing in the restore looks for a face to redraw it. The face goes through the same general model as the wallpaper behind it. Enhance, the default, reduces noise, grain and compression blocks at 2× or 4×. Check the result against the face the camera recorded.

Two limits. A face that was tiny, blurred or out of focus on the tape stays soft, because the detail was never recorded and Enhance only works with what is there (why upscaling can't fix focus). And Max, the slow generative lane in Pro, rebuilds detail across the whole frame, faces included. The right-hand crop shows the cost: the eye is sharper, and the stubble the film recorded is mostly gone. Use Max for clothes, rooms and the birthday cake. On a face you know, compare it with Enhance before you keep it.

Old home video questions

Can Crisp capture a VHS tape?

No. Get a digital file with a USB capture device or a transfer service first. Then open that file in Crisp on Mac or PC.

Can I try an old home video without buying Pro?

Yes. Trim to a piece up to 15 seconds long. Free enhanced video exports stop at 15 seconds and carry a small "crispvideo.app" mark. Pro removes the mark and limit.

Will it change my family's faces?

Enhance has no face model. It treats a face like the rest of the frame. A tiny or out-of-focus face stays soft. Max on Mac rebuilds detail across the frame, including faces, so compare it with Enhance before keeping a result.
