Why it's worth a minute

Family videos can show faces, homes and places you would rather keep to yourself, so it is worth knowing where a copy will be held. Help Net Security reported on 21 September 2026, read 20 November 2026, that attackers “exploited a vulnerability in its image upload server, stealing approximately 23.62 million user records”. Image metadata from “approximately 490 million records” included “EXIF location data where present, OCR text extracted from images”. Gyazo is a screenshot-sharing service, not a video upscaler. The point is general: whatever you upload is held somewhere.

What Crisp does, measured

On 20 November 2026 we ran a 2× restore of a 20-second clip in Crisp 1.0.2089 on a Mac and logged every connection it made to anything outside the computer. During the 31.6-second job Crisp sent 0 bytes off the Mac.

This was one job with one clip, using a Pro licence on an M1 Ultra Mac. We used nettop to count traffic on outside network interfaces before and after the job, and lsof to check for outside connections every two seconds. Traffic between the app window and its engine on the same Mac was left out.

Crisp does use the internet for:

  • Checking for updates.
  • Licence activation and a weekly re-check.
  • The sale-price check on a free copy.
  • The one-time download of the Max model.

None of these carry your video.

Check any app yourself

On a Mac, open Activity Monitor, choose Network and watch “Sent Bytes” for the app while it works on a video. On Windows, open Resource Monitor and choose Network to see each process's network activity while the app works.

These views show network activity, not what a connection contains. An update check can send bytes too. Watch the app before and during the job and compare that activity with what its policy says.
